# Neural Polar Codes for Source Coding 

Bashar Huleihel Ziv Aharoni Haim H. Permuter Ben-Gurion University Duke University Ben-Gurion University basharh@post.bgu.ac.il ziv.aharoni@duke.edu haimp@bgu.ac.il 

Abstract 

This paper presents a data-driven approach to lossless and lossy source compression using polar codes. Classical polar source coding achieves fundamental compression limits with low complexity for binary memoryless sources. For unknown q-ary sources with memory, however, successive-cancellation decoding requires an explicit source model, and its complexity grows rapidly with alphabet and state-space sizes. For comparison, we present analytical successive-cancellation recursions and show that exact implementations scale quadratically with alphabet size and cubically with state-space size. Motivated by these bottlenecks, we employ neural polar decoders trained directly from data, eliminating the need for an explicit source model and reducing decoding complexity. Once trained, the neural polar decoders approximate conditional distributions of the synthetic polar coordinates. These learned distributions are used both to estimate information-theoretic quantities, including entropy rates, mutual information, and rate–distortion functions, and to construct explicit polar compression schemes. For lossless compression, the neural polar decoder identifies high-entropy polarized coordinates and reconstructs the remaining coordinates through the learned successive-cancellation recursion. For lossy compression, we combine neural polar decoders with a neural test channel to estimate and optimize the mutual-information–distortion tradeoff and construct an explicit lossy polar code. We further extend the framework to the Wyner–Ziv setting with decoder side information by learning conditional polarized distributions and a reconstruction mapping. Experiments on memoryless sources, sources with memory, and real genomic data show that the learned decoders closely match analytical successive-cancellation decoders when available and remain applicable when classical polar methods are computationally infeasible or the required source model is unavailable.

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Source coding studies the efficient compression of data generated by a source, requiring exact reconstruc-tion in the lossless setting and reconstruction within a prescribed distortion in the lossy setting. In lossless compression, the entropy characterizes the fundamental limit for memoryless sources, and the entropy rate plays the corresponding role for stationary sources with memory. Classical model-based schemes, such as Huffman coding [1] and arithmetic coding [2], approach these limits when supplied with an accurate source model, for example through block coding or context-dependent conditional probabilities, whereas universal schemes such as Lempel–Ziv coding [3] do not require prior knowledge of the source distribution and are asymptotically optimal for finite-alphabet stationary ergodic sources. The latter guarantees are asymptotic, however, and finite-block performance can depend strongly on the available sequence length and on the source structure. For lossy compression, rate–distortion theory characterizes the minimum rate required to reproduce a source within a specified average distortion [4]–[5]. Despite these foundations, translating information-theoretic limits into tractable coding algorithms remains challenging for many real sources, which may be non-binary, statistically dependent, and structured across time or space, as in text, genomic sequences, images, and scientific measurements. In such regimes the source law is often unknown, and even when a finite-state or high-order model is available, the inference required by exact model-based coding can be computationally prohibitive. arXiv:2610.13554v1 [cs.IT] 9 Oct 2026 For binary memoryless sources, polar codes provide constructive coding schemes that achieve the information-theoretic limits with low-complexity encoding and decoding. Following Arıkan’s channel-polarization construction [6], polar methods have been developed for both lossless and lossy source coding [7]–[10]. The polar transform maps a length-N source block to synthetic coordinates whose conditional entropies polarize: most coordinates become nearly deterministic given the preceding ones, while the remaining coordinates carry essentially all of the source uncertainty. Compression is performed by storing the high-entropy coordinates, and reconstruction is carried out by successive-cancellation (SC) decoding using the source distribution. For sources with memory, SC decoding can be implemented through successive-cancellation trellis (SCT) recursions, which augment the polar recursion with trellis state tracking [11]. For binary memoryless sources the SC updates reduce to simple scalar recursions. For non-binary finite-field alphabets, however, the likelihood recursions become vector-valued, and for finite-state sources they require state tracking, so that the complexity grows rapidly with the alphabet size and the number of source states. Neural networks (NNs) provide a complementary approach by learning expressive statistical mod-els directly from data. Autoregressive models, variational autoencoders (VAEs), normalizing flows, and generative-adversarial networks (GANs) have been used to model complex distributions and, in several compression settings, to parameterize encoders, decoders, or test channels [12]–[17]. Neural decoders for polar and related codes, as well as learned distributed compressors, have also been studied, including neural belief-propagation, graph-neural-network, neural successive-cancellation, and learned-binning ap-proaches [18]–[29]. These works primarily address channel coding or settings in which the underlying channel or source model is relatively simple. The present work instead develops a data-driven polar source-coding architecture for finite-alphabet sources, sources with memory, and real-world sources for which analytical SC operations are unavailable or computationally impractical. In this paper, we develop a neural–polar framework for source coding that combines the structure of polar compression with data-driven estimation. The approach preserves the polar transform and the SC computation graph, while replacing the analytical core operations of SC decoding, namely the check-node and bit-node updates, with neural modules trained directly from samples. Building on the neural polar decoding framework originally developed for binary-input channel coding [30]–[32], we extend neural polar decoders (NPDs) to finite-alphabet source coding. The framework therefore does not require an explicit probabilistic source model, and its recursive computational cost is governed by the chosen neural architecture and the depth of the polar recursion rather than by the quadratic alphabet enumeration of analytical SC recursions or the cubic state-space dependence of analytical SCT recursions. We focus on single-encoder, single-decoder problems, including Wyner–Ziv coding with decoder-only side information. Extending the framework to multiterminal source coding introduces additional challenges. For example, Slepian–Wolf coding separately encodes correlated sources and jointly decodes them. Its objective is a rate region rather than a single rate, requiring encoder-specific transmitted sets and a joint decoding rule for the desired rate tuple [33]–[34]. The main contributions are summarized as follows.  

> •

Lossless compression. We extend the NPD methodology from binary-input channel decoding to finite-alphabet source coding over memoryless and finite-state sources. The learned decoder estimates the polarized conditional distributions required for entropy estimation, synthetic-coordinate selection, and SC reconstruction, so that the encoder can select the high-entropy coordinates and the decoder can reconstruct the remaining ones through the learned SC recursion. In the experiments, the NPD closely reproduces the behavior of the analytical SC or SCT decoder whenever the latter is tractable. On the 

E. coli benchmark [35]–[36], supplying the sequential NPD probabilities to an arithmetic coder yields a lossless rate of 1.883 bits per base, comparable to the NNCP rate reported in [37]. This experiment is included to demonstrate applicability to real-world data.  

> •

Lossy compression. We develop a data-driven variational procedure for lossy source coding in which a neural test channel is optimized against a multi-letter rate–distortion objective [4]–[5], [8]. For a 2fixed test channel, two NPDs estimate the entropy of the polarized reconstruction sequence and its conditional entropy given the source sequence. Their difference is a sample-based estimate of the induced mutual information, following the NPD-based estimator of [31]. The test channel is then updated with a distortion penalty through a score-function gradient. The procedure produces estimated rate–distortion operating points, and the same learned polarized statistics specify an explicit polar lossy coding scheme.  

> •

Wyner–Ziv coding. We extend this estimation–optimization principle to source coding with decoder side information in the Wyner–Ziv setting [38]. Polar codes have previously been applied to this problem through nested constructions for binary memoryless models, where the direct construction attains points on the auxiliary Wyner–Ziv functional and the full tradeoff is obtained by convexi-fication, e.g., through time sharing [8], [34]. Our formulation jointly learns a multi-letter auxiliary test channel, a reconstruction mapping, and the conditional polarized distributions that define the conditional mutual-information term. The rate-carrying set consists of the synthetic coordinates that are difficult to infer from the side information but predictable from the source through the learned test channel. To motivate our approach, we establish a nested polar coding theorem in the setting where the induced joint processes admit finite-state, aperiodic, irreducible Markov (FAIM) representations, building on the conditional polarization results of [39]. Together, these contributions yield a unified neural–polar approach for estimating source-coding limits and constructing lossless, lossy, and Wyner–Ziv codes in regimes where the source law is unknown or the corresponding analytical SC/SCT recursions are computationally prohibitive. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section II introduces the notation and the polar source-coding preliminaries used throughout the paper. Section III states the analytical SC and SCT recursions for non-binary alphabets and finite-state sources and identifies their complexity with respect to the alphabet and state-space sizes. Section IV presents the NPD-based source-coding architecture together with the proposed lossless compression scheme. Section V develops the lossy compression framework based on NPD mutual-information estimation and neural test-channel optimization. Section VI extends the construction to the Wyner–Ziv setting with decoder side information. Section VII concludes the paper. Remark 1. In contrast to analytical SC-based polar decoders, which require explicit knowledge of the source model to compute the relevant conditional distributions, the NPD learns the decoding operations directly from samples. Moreover, as shown in [30, Thm. 4], for a fixed internal neural architecture with hidden width k and embedding dimension d, the recursive part of its computational complexity is O (kdN log N ), with no dependence on |S| . In the non-binary setting, the softmax head Hθ adds a linear dependence on |X | through its output logits when evaluated at the leaves, while the NPD still avoids the explicit |X | 2 alphabet enumeration and |S| 3 state-space enumeration required by the analytical SC/SCT recursions of Section III.

W YNER –Z IV VIA NPD In this section, we extend the NPD-based rate–distortion estimation optimization framework of Section V to the Wyner–Ziv setting, where side information is available only at the decoder. Unlike the ordinary rate–distortion problem, the Wyner–Ziv formulation involves an auxiliary random variable, the rate is characterized by a conditional mutual information, and the reconstruction is formed at the decoder from the auxiliary description and the side information. Consequently, the learning procedure must optimize both a test channel that generates the auxiliary representation and a reconstruction mapping, while the NPDs estimate the synthetic conditional entropy terms that define the conditional mutual information. The scheme applies to stationary ergodic source pairs and is based on the multi-letter Wyner–Ziv rate– distortion functional, whereas its polar-coding guarantees are established for the FAIM setting under the 19 assumptions of Lemma 3 and Theorem 2. Throughout this section, we define explicitly the components that differ from Section V and refer to the corresponding definitions there for the common structure.

C ONCLUSIONS AND FUTURE WORK 

This paper introduced a data-driven polar-coding framework for source coding based on neural polar decoders. The main idea is to replace the explicit successive-cancellation update rules of classical polar constructions with trainable modules that estimate the relevant polarized conditional distributions directly from samples. This yields a unified approach to lossless compression, lossy compression, and Wyner–Ziv coding, while avoiding the explicit quadratic alphabet enumeration and cubic state-space enumeration that limit analytical SC and SCT decoders for large alphabets and sources with memory. The proposed schemes retain the recursive polar structure and use NPDs to identify the polarized coordinates required for compression and reconstruction. In the lossy setting, NPDs were combined with a neural test channel to optimize rate–distortion operating points from data, and the Wyner–Ziv extension incorporated decoder side information through learned conditional polarized distributions and a learned reconstruction mapping, supported by conditional polarization and nested polar coding results for FAIM processes. The numerical results show that, when analytical benchmarks are available, the learned constructions closely match them, and that the same methodology remains applicable in settings where explicit source models or analytical decoding rules are unavailable. An important direction for future work is extending the NPD framework to multiterminal source coding. A representative example is Slepian–Wolf coding, which requires learning coupled synthetic conditional distributions and constructing encoder-specific transmitted sets and a joint decoder for target rate tuples, while each encoder uses only its local observation.
