# Goal-Oriented Ordered Autoencoding for Successive Refinement in Progressive Communication 

Qi Liao, Dario Bega 

Nokia Bell Labs, Stuttgart, Germany E-Mail: {qi.liao,dario.bega }@nokia-bell-labs.com 

Abstract —Goal-oriented communication often struggles to sus-tain downstream task performance under variable and unpre-dictable bandwidth. We address this problem by proposing GO-OAE (Goal-Oriented Ordered AutoEncoding), which learns a progressively refinable quantized latent sequence, optimized directly for task utility rather than source reconstruction. Each component of the sequence is represented for transmission by its code index, and the learned ordering enables inference from any received prefix of the index sequence, with performance improving as more indices arrive. We prove that optimizing the goal-oriented distortion objective is equivalent to solving a weighted conditional information bottleneck formulation, and introduce an unbiased multi-prefix importance sampling (MPIS) estimator, to reduce GO-OAE training computational cost from 

O(BK ) to O(Bm ) with m≪K, and reduces the estimator variance by a factor of 1/m . Experiments show that, on Mini-ImageNet, GO-OAE achieves over 96% of the lossless upper-bound classification accuracy using 324 bits per image ( 683 ×

compression vs. raw RGB), and already attains over 94% of the upper bound with only 70% of the transmitted indices. It consis-tently outperforms reconstruction-optimized codecs and ordered autoencoders trained with uniform prefix sampling across all transmission budgets, demonstrating effective communication at extreme compression rates. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Next-generation communication systems increasingly sup-port machine intelligence rather than human perception, with transmitted data used for downstream tasks such as classi-fication, detection, and decision-making. This shift calls for coding methods that preserve task-relevant information under bandwidth constraints, rather than merely minimizing pixel-level distortion. Although goal-oriented communication [1] captures this principle, it has yet to unify compression and inference while ensuring consistent downstream performance across varying and uncertain bandwidth constraints. A common approach is to use generative neural codecs [2, 3], such as variational autoencoder (VAE) [4], for com-pression followed by a separately trained task model. This modular “compress first, infer later” pipeline assumes that reconstruction fidelity preserves task-critical information. Yet our results in Section VI-C1 challenge this: even with good reconstruction quality, classification accuracy on reconstructed images remains far below the raw-image bound ( 18 .9% vs. 

77 .5% ), motivating goal-oriented codec-task co-design .Beyond the modular versus joint design question, practical communication systems can benefit from progressive transmis-sion [5], where an ordered latent sequence supports inference across varying bandwidths without re-encoding. The ordered autoencoder (OAE) introduced by [6] enables progressively improved reconstruction from increasingly long prefixes of the latent sequence. However, existing OAEs are tied to reconstruction and cannot recover task-relevant information. These observations expose a key research gap: existing frameworks lack an information-theoretic approach for learn-ing emergent ordered discrete representations, whose prefixes are directly optimized for downstream tasks. Addressing this requires (i) a goal-oriented objective that preserves progres-sive structure, (ii) an efficient training method that avoids evaluating all prefix lengths, and (iii) semantic regularization to maintain discriminative information in the codebook. We address these challenges with goal-oriented ordered autoen-coder (GO-OAE) (Figure 1). GO-OAE jointly optimizes the encoder, vector quantizer, and task decoder through an unbi-ased importance-sampled multi-prefix objective. The resulting representations prioritize task-relevant information in earlier components without explicit architectural ordering constraints, enabling variable-bandwidth communication. Goal-Oriented Semantic Communication. Semantic com-munication shifts the objective from reliable bit transmission to preserving task-relevant information [10]. Existing studies have investigated its information-theoretic foundations, AI-enabled architectures, end-to-end learning methods, and poten-tial applications to future networks [11, 12, 13]. Recent works have increasingly incorporated generative models into image and video semantic communication. Diffusion-based methods use semantic extraction and generative reconstruction to im-prove perceptual robustness under channel impairments [14]. Self-supervised approaches learn task-agnostic latent represen-tations that are reconstructed before classification [15], while multi-task frameworks jointly support image reconstruction and classification from shared semantic features [16]. Despite these advances, most existing methods remain reconstruction-oriented: task inference is performed on re-constructed signals, and progressive transmission primarily improves perceptual fidelity. Moreover, hierarchical represen-tations do not necessarily induce an ordered latent space in which every prefix is directly optimized for task utility. 

Ordered Autoencoding and Successive Refinement. Suc-cessive refinement is a fundamental concept in information theory, allowing progressive transmission of information with increasing levels of detail [5]. Learned OAEs similarly order latent variables so that longer prefixes improve reconstruction [6], while autoregressive methods predict the missing suffix from the received prefix [17]. However, these approaches optimize reconstruction or generative completion rather than task utility. Let z≤k denote the transmitted prefix with length 

k, and bz>k ∼ p (bz>k |z≤k) an autoregressively generated suffix. The suffix is generated solely from the received pre-fix, i.e., I (y; z≤k, bz>k ) = I (y; z≤k) . Hence, autoregressive approaches learn a conditional prior over the untransmitted latent suffix, enabling plausible latent completion. However, it cannot add task information beyond the received prefix and also incurs sequential decoding overhead. Another relevant work is Matryoshka representation learn-ing (MRL) [18], which learns nested prefixes of a single final embedding after full backbone processing, placing most com-putation at the transmitter. It targets scalable representation storage and inference rather than lightweight-device compu-tation and communication reduction. In contrast, GO-OAE orders and discretizes intermediate latent sequences before full semantic reasoning, enabling a lightweight transmitter and progressive receiver-side inference. III. B. Vector Quantized Autoencoder 

We adopt VQ-AE variant of vector quantized variational autoencoder (VQ-VAE) [19] as the underlying discrete latent modeling framework for ordered autoencoding. VQ-AE learns discrete latent space by mapping the continuous encoder outputs to a finite set of learnable codebook embeddings through vector quantization. The resulting codebook indices are mapped back to their corresponding embeddings, from which the decoder reconstructs the input. We choose VQ-AE because it naturally supports ordered and progressively refinable latent sequence. First, discretized latent spaces provide more stable and semantically structured representations than continuous variables under progressive truncation, where discrete codebook indices can be trans-mitted or decoded in an ordered manner. Second, transmit-ting discrete indices is far more communication-efficient and implementation-friendly than continuous latent vectors. For example, Llama 3 8B model [20] represents each token with a 4096 -dimensional FP16 vector, requiring 65 , 536 bits per token, whereas indexing a codebook of size 128 , 256 requires only 17 bits. In vision tasks, even smaller codebooks are typ-ically sufficient, e.g., 512 entries, with each latent component index (or ”code index”) represented by only 9 bits. To mitigate this, we adopt the exponential moving average (EMA)-based VQ-VAE [21] update scheme for our autoen-coder, which updates codebook embedding using exponential moving average of assigned encoder outputs. Remark 2 (Information Bottleneck Behavior and Latent Or-dering) . Even without explicitly including I(z; x) in (8) , the GO-OAE objective exhibits an implicit information bottleneck effect through the VQ-AE bottleneck, finite codebook, latent dimensionality, and stochastic truncation. A. Datasets 

We evaluate the proposed GO-OAE framework on Mini-ImageNet [8, 9], a widely used fine-grained classification benchmark consisting of 100 classes. We use the public 

timm/mini-imagenet split via HuggingFace [23]: ∼50k samples for training, ∼10k samples for validation, and ∼5k samples for testing. All 100 classes appear in every split with consistent label indices. Images are resized to 96 ×96 (to create 

36 spatial patches of size 16 ×16 ) and normalized to [0 , 1] .Training augmentation includes random crop, horizontal flip, color jitter, rotation, MixUp ( α=0 .2), and light blurring and noise corruption. Validation uses center resize and ImageNet normalization only. Top-1 accuracy on the validation set is the primary goal-oriented metric. 

B. Network Architecture and Training Details 

We conducted experiments using both convolutional neural network (CNN)- and vision transformer (ViT)-based back-bones under the same full-rate budget of 324 bits per image. Due to space limitations, we focus here on the lighter CNN-based model and leave the backbone discussion, including insights into ViT-based models, to Section VI-D. 

CNN backbone. The encoder maps each image to a

36 ×6×6 latent tensor, through four strided convolutional stages ( cnn_factor=2 , channels 128 →256 →512 ), a resid-ual block, a final 3×3 convolution projecting to 36 chan-nels, and adaptive pooling. The receiver feeds the (possibly truncated) quantized tensor to a ResNet-style classification head: 3×3 convolutions, batch norm, ReLU, two residual blocks, two stride-2 downsamples, average pooling, and a linear classifier, yielding class logits. 

ViT backbone. We use a split ViT-Small backbone (resized to 96 ×96 , yielding N =36 patch tokens of dimension 

384 ). The transmitter applies the first E=4 transformer blocks, followed by a 2-layer perceiver ( 36 queries, 8 heads) to a latent dimension Dz =128 , then vector-quantized. The receiver maps quantized latent components back to 384 -dimensional token embeddings, expands to 36 spatial tokens with a 2-layer perceiver, and runs the remaining 8 ViT blocks plus the head with separate CLS and positional embeddings. Classification is performed from the final CLS representation. The CNN-based model contains about 8M, 0.02 M, and 

3.3M parameters in the encoder, VQ codebook, and decoder, respectively. The corresponding parameter counts for the ViT-based model are about 11 M, 0.07 M, and 17 .9M. Unless otherwise specified, both architectures use a trainable EMA codebook with size N =512 (9 bits per quantized latent

D. Backbone Model Discussion 

Our experiments in Figure 7 show that GO-OAE interacts differently with CNN and ViT backbones. CNN’s feature specialization supports complementary latent information and clear progressive refinement. In contrast, standard ViTs are optimized for early global contextualization, which conflicts with ordered representation learning: task-relevant information becomes rapidly mixed and redundantly distributed across tokens, allowing the first few latent components to capture most of the utility and yielding nearly flat prefix-performance curves. Using fewer transformer blocks before quantization (E=1 instead of E=4 ) spreads task utility across more latent components, yielding progressive gains over longer prefix. However, the reduced depth at transmitter also limits task-relevant information extraction before the bottleneck, resulting in lower converged accuracy. Achieving progressive refine-ment with transformer backbones may therefore require ar-chitectural redesigns that preserve decomposable intermediate features and defer global integration until after the communi-cation bottleneck. This represents an important direction for future work. VII. C ONCLUSIONS 

We introduced GO-OAE, a goal-oriented progressive com-munication framework robust to unpredictable bandwidth that learns ordered quantized representations, enabling flex-ible task inference rather than source reconstruction. We showed that the goal-oriented distortion objective admits a weighted conditional information bottleneck interpretation, and introduced MPIS to cut training cost from O(BK ) to 

O(Bm ), with m≪K, and with variance scaling as 1/m . On MiniImageNet, our approach attains ≥96% of the lossless bound at 324 bits/image (683 × compression) and ≥94% 

with only 70% of the quantized latent components, outper-forming reconstruction-based codecs and OAEs trained with uniform prefix sampling at all budgets. Semantic preservation regularization and ordered importance sampling are essential when the communication budget is tight. Future work will extend GO-OAE to drone surveillance and robotic vision under realistic communication, compute, and energy constraints, while developing transformer architectures better suited to progressive refinement.
