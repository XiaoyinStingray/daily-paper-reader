ABSTRACT
Recently, unified high-definition image restoration has attracted considerable at-
tention; however, existing models tend to excessively increase their depth in pur-
suit of improved generalization, which often yields only limited gains. Meanwhile,
loop-based learning paradigms have drawn widespread attention due to their low
parameter counts and strong regression capability, as exemplified by GPT-6 and
looped Transformers. In this paper, we introduce the loop learning paradigm to
address restoration tasks that require cross-domain learning. Specifically, we pro-
pose LoopMoEVR, a loop-based mixture-of-experts model capable of handling
degraded ultra-high-definition (UHD) inputs.
First, a degradation-conditioned
low-rank loop embedding is designed to construct input-dependent stage condi-
tions. Second, a spatio-temporal iterative adaptive normalization module, termed
IterAda3DN, is developed to fuse local features with global loop context, thereby
performing position-wise aﬀine modulation. Finally, the expert branches further
integrate the attention-updated local and global video states with the loop con-
ditions to generate dedicated modulation parameters, while an input-conditioned
depth predictor adaptively configures the number of loop iterations. With only
approximately 0.884M trainable parameters, the proposed model uniformly han-
dles UHD video dehazing, deraining, denoising, and low-light enhancement tasks,
achieving state-of-the-art restoration performance on both public benchmarks and
real-world scenarios.

INTRODUCTION
Unified video restoration aims to address multiple degradations with a single model while exploiting
cross-frame information to recover details and maintain temporal consistency. Extending unified
restoration to ultra-high-definition (UHD) videos introduces further challenges. A key challenge is
to increase restoration depth while retaining the ability to adapt to different degradations and evolving
feature states.
For unified UHD video restoration, MoCRA (Wang et al., 2026b) introduces UHV-4K-AIO, built
from 100 clean 4K clips. It allocates conditioning capacity between a coarse branch and a full-
resolution refiner according to degradation frequency, enabling native 4K restoration without optical
flow using approximately 3.6M parameters. However, its frame-wise conditions do not iteratively
refine intermediate representations, its fixed inference depth cannot adapt to degradation severity, and
it does not explicitly model global video states or cross-iteration context. Unified UHD restoration
therefore still calls for progressive refinement and adaptive depth within a limited parameter budget.
The loop learning paradigm offers a way to address these challenges by progressively refining restora-
tion results over multiple iterations. The Universal Transformer (Dehghani et al., 2019) repeatedly

iteration-dependent modulation (Saunshi et al., 2025; Xu & Sato, 2025). SMELT further investigates
looped MoE models under matched resource budgets, demonstrating the effectiveness of looped com-
putation for language modeling (Wang et al., 2026a). LoopMoE combines looped computation with
sparse experts and generates modulation parameters from iteration position and current local hidden
states through IterAdaLN (Chen et al., 2026). However, its base iteration embeddings are determined
by the iteration index without explicit input-degradation conditions, and its modulation pathways do
not directly integrate global video states or cross-iteration context. In unified UHD video restoration,
the same iteration position may correspond to different restoration needs under different degrada-
tions, while spatio-temporal variation within a video requires local updates to incorporate clip-level
state and loop context. We therefore construct a conditioning mechanism driven jointly by degrada-
tion, iteration stage, and video state, while aligning expert modulation with local features updated by
attention, to support iterative restoration across degradation conditions.
To this end, we propose LoopMoEVR, a loop-based mixture-of-experts model for unified UHD video
restoration that incorporates degradation conditions and video-state information into iterative restora-
tion. The model comprises input encoding, looped latent-feature updates, and output reconstruction.
First, degradation-conditioned low-rank loop embeddings combine input degradation representa-
tions, a severity-related scalar, and iteration position to generate input-dependent conditions for each
restoration stage. Second, the spatio-temporal iterative adaptive normalization module, IterAda3DN,
integrates local spatio-temporal features, global video states, and cross-iteration context to modulate
features at each position. The expert branch further uses local and global video states updated by
attention to condition expert transformations. In addition, a depth predictor selects the number of it-
erations according to the input, adapting the restoration process to different computational needs. By
repeatedly applying spatio-temporal processing layers, LoopMoEVR performs multi-stage restora-
tion with approximately 0.884M parameters. At the same network width and four-stage execution
depth, it uses 71.6% fewer parameters than a model with separate backbone parameters for each
stage, with a PSNR difference of only 0.08 dB. This result shows that loop-based refinement achieves
restoration quality close to that of stage-specific parameterization with a substantially more compact
model, supporting our choice of a loop-based restoration architecture. Figure 1 compares the mean
PSNR, parameter count, and reported inference time of LoopMoEVR and competing methods. Fig-
ure 2 shows a smartphone-based 4K video restoration example.
Our main contributions are as follows:
1. We propose LoopMoEVR for unified UHD video restoration, introducing the loop learning
paradigm to progressively refine video features. By conditioning loop embeddings on input
degradation representations, severity information, and iteration position, the model adapts
feature updates to different degradation conditions and restoration stages.
2


2. We design IterAda3DN to integrate local spatio-temporal features with global loop con-
text for position-wise aﬀine modulation. Local and global video states updated by atten-
tion further provide dedicated modulation parameters for the expert branch, while an input-
conditioned depth predictor assigns an appropriate loop depth to each input.
3. With approximately 0.884M trainable parameters, LoopMoEVR jointly addresses UHD
video dehazing, deraining, denoising, and low-light enhancement. It achieves the highest
mean PSNR on UHV-4K-AIO and performs well in evaluations of time-varying degrada-
tions and temporal consistency.

RELATED WORK
2.1
UNIFIED VIDEO RESTORATION
Unified video restoration combines temporal information with a shared model across degradations.
AverNet (Zhao et al., 2024), UniWRV (Wan et al., 2026), and ORCANet (Li et al., 2026) use align-
ment, weather priors, and clip- or frame-level prompts, respectively; MoCRA (Wang et al., 2026b)
targets native 4K restoration through frequency-aware, rank-one conditioning. In degradation-aware
image restoration, AirNet (Li et al., 2022), PromptIR (Potlapalli et al., 2023), and UtilityIR (Chen
& Pei, 2025) adapt features through learned representations or prompts; AdaIR (Cui et al., 2025)
uses frequency cues, while MoCE-IR (Zamfir et al., 2025) and UniRestorer (Lin et al., 2026) study
expert selection. UniLDiff (Cheng et al., 2026) incorporates degradation features into denoising,
RONIN (Janjua et al., 2026) uses language-based degradation semantics, and video methods such
as CDUN (Cheng et al., 2023) and LaverNet (Zhao et al., 2025) study adaptive updates and se-
lective propagation. These approaches motivate input-aware restoration; our focus is degradation-
conditioned, parameter-shared refinement of UHD video.
2.2
LOOPED DEEP LEARNING
Looped models increase effective depth by reusing computation, as in the Universal Transformer (De-
hghani et al., 2019), Huginn (Geiping et al., 2025), ELT (Goyal et al., 2026), and Looped World Mod-
els (Lu et al., 2026). Studies of loop expressivity (Saunshi et al., 2025) and timestep encoding (Xu
& Sato, 2025) examine repeated transformations, while LoopFormer (Jeddi et al., 2026) conditions
updates on iteration step size and Mixture-of-Recursions (Bae et al., 2025) routes token-level depth.
LoopMoE (Chen et al., 2026) combines sparse experts with iteration- and local-state modulation;
its expert branch already uses the attention-updated state. SMELT (Wang et al., 2026a) evaluates
looped MoE under matched resource budgets. Building on this work, LoopMoEVR conditions loop
embeddings on input degradation, incorporates global video state and cross-iteration context into
modulation, and selects depth according to the input.

3.1
OVERALL FRAMEWORK
Figure 3 illustrates the overall mechanism of LoopMoEVR. Following the shared-computation set-
ting of LoopMoE (Chen et al., 2026), given a degraded video clip X, LoopMoEVR extracts latent
features and degradation conditions, then iteratively updates video features through degradation-
conditioned loop embeddings, spatio-temporal iterative adaptive normalization, and state-aware ex-
pert modulation. An input-conditioned depth predictor selects the iteration count from {3, 4, 5}, and
a decoder fuses the final and shallow features to reconstruct ˆY.

3.3
DEGRADATION-CONDITIONED LOW-RANK LOOP EMBEDDING
The degradation representation z and scalar s describe restoration needs but do not distinguish loop
stages. We therefore combine them with the sinusoidal position encoding (Vaswani et al., 2017) pτ
of iteration τ, correcting the base iteration embedding through a low-rank residual. First, we generate
degradation and stage encodings gdeg and gτ:
gdeg = tanh(A[z; s] + a) ,
gτ = tanh(Bpτ + b) ,
(2)
where [z; s] denotes concatenation, A, B are projection matrices, and a, b are biases. Both encodings
lie in Rr: gdeg remains constant within a clip, while gτ varies across stages.
Low-rank element-wise interactions have a precedent in bilinear pooling (Kim et al., 2017). Here,
their element-wise interaction generates a conditional residual ∆eτ, which is added to the base em-
bedding Gp(pτ) to obtain the current loop condition eτ:
∆eτ = U(gdeg ⊙gτ) ,
eτ = Gp(pτ) + ∆eτ,
(3)
where ⊙denotes element-wise multiplication, and U ∈Rde×r maps the interaction into the em-
bedding space. Choosing r < de restricts the correction to a low-dimensional subspace, limiting
additional parameters.
This interaction makes degradation corrections stage-dependent, providing conditions for different
inputs and iterations. Zero-initializing U preserves the initial base embedding, while training learns
degradation-dependent residuals. The next module combines eτ with video state and loop history
for position-wise modulation.

3.6
OUTPUT RECONSTRUCTION AND DYNAMIC DEPTH SCHEDULING
After K refinement iterations, the decoder D restores hK to the input resolution. The residual fusion
network Ffuse combines it with shallow features hs to reconstruct ˆY:
ˆY = clip[0,1](X + Ffuse([D(hK); hs])) ,
(12)
where features are cropped to the original size before fusion. The output preserves the input temporal
length and spatial resolution and is clipped to [0, 1].
To adapt computation to the input, the depth predictor maps [z; s] to probabilities p4, p5 of requiring
at least four or five iterations, progressively determining K:
K = 3 + I[p4 ≥0.5] + I[p4 ≥0.5 ∧p5 ≥0.5],
(13)
where I is the indicator function, and p4, p5 are Sigmoid outputs of predicted logits. This selects
K ∈{3, 4, 5}, permitting five iterations only when the four-iteration condition holds.

EXPERIMENTS
4.1
EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
Data and evaluation. We use the oﬀicial UHV-4K-AIO split (Wang et al., 2026b) and one model
for all four restoration tasks. Five-frame windows are evaluated on their center frames at native
3840 × 2160 resolution. We report restoration quality and temporal consistency metrics with equal
weighting across tasks; dataset splits, metric definitions, and evaluation protocols are detailed in
Appendix B.1–B.3.
Implementation and comparisons. Training uses AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019) and FP16
mixed precision (Micikevicius et al., 2018) on one NVIDIA V100 (32GB), with aligned 256 × 256
crops, a batch size of 2, gradient clipping, and cosine learning rate decay with warmup. The restora-
tion network is first trained for 100 epochs with four iterations. The restoration network and condition
extraction module are then frozen, depth labels are generated as detailed in Appendix A.2, and the
depth predictor is trained for another 100 epochs. We compare methods on the oﬀicial UHV-4K-AIO
test split using the same evaluation metrics.

4.3
ROBUSTNESS TO TIME-VARYING DEGRADATIONS
To evaluate robustness to within-clip degradation changes, we switch degradation types every ∆t ∈
{6, 3} frames in UHV-4K-AIO test clips. LoopMoEVR achieves 27.68 and 27.85 dB mean PSNR,
respectively, at most 0.19 dB below the static setting, indicating robustness to within-clip degradation
changes; complete results appear in Appendix B.4.
4.4
TEMPORAL STABILITY
LoopMoEVR obtains a tLPIPS of 0.1182 and a Warping Error of 0.0297, compared with Aver-
Net’s 0.1238 and 0.0296, respectively. The full comparison appears in Appendix B.4. These results
demonstrate that the compact model maintains competitive inter-frame consistency at both levels.
7


Temporal difference supervision constrains inter-frame changes during training, while video-state
modulation incorporates within-clip spatio-temporal information during restoration. These mecha-
nisms address temporal consistency through the optimization objective and feature updates, respec-
tively. Together with the main results, the evaluation shows that LoopMoEVR improves average
restoration quality while maintaining competitive temporal stability.
4.5
ANALYSIS OF NON-UHD RESOLUTION RESULTS
LoopMoEVR remains effective at non-UHD resolutions but shows a smaller advantage than in UHD
restoration (Appendix B.5). Designed for UHD inputs, its looped refinement exploits rich structural
and degradation cues; fewer high-frequency details at lower resolutions limit iterative gains.
4.6
REAL-WORLD UHD IMAGE RESTORATION
We apply LoopMoEVR trained on UHV-4K-AIO directly to real-world UHD images. As shown
in Appendix D.4, it suppresses real-world degradations while preserving clear local structures and
texture details, achieving the best visual restoration quality among the compared methods.

DISCUSSION: WHY DOES LOOPED COMPUTATION BENEFIT UNIFIED UHD VIDEO
RESTORATION?
Unified 4K restoration must address haze and low light visible at reduced resolution, alongside rain
and noise requiring native-scale information (Wang et al., 2026b; Liu et al., 2025). Backbone reuse
increases processing depth with shared parameters (Dehghani et al., 2019):
hτ = Fθ(hτ−1; eτ, mτ),
Pshared ≈Pθ,
Pindependent ≈KPθ,
Here Fθ updates video features conditioned on loop embedding eτ and momentum state mτ. Its Pθ
backbone parameters are reused over K iterations, whereas independent backbones require approx-
imately KPθ parameters. LoopMoEVR refines compact spatio-temporal features and fuses native-
resolution shallow features for 4K reconstruction.
Figure 6: Iterative dehazing results of LoopMoEVR.
Degradation, video-state, and iteration conditions adapt shared-backbone updates to the restoration
state; prior work supports the role of timestep encoding (Xu & Sato, 2025). Figure 6 illustrates pro-
gressive correction, and Appendix D.3 shows that the normal condition order outperforms reversed,
shuffled, and repeated alternatives. The parameter-sharing comparison appears in Appendix C.7.
LoopMoEVR combines state-dependent refinement with native-resolution reconstruction to enable
unified 4K restoration with a compact model.

CONCLUSION
We propose LoopMoEVR, a unified UHD video restoration model based on the loop learning
paradigm. It combines degradation-conditioned low-rank loop embeddings, IterAda3DN, and state-
aware expert modulation to enable unified UHD video restoration with adaptive depth. With approx-
imately 0.884M parameters, the model achieves the highest mean PSNR on UHV-4K-AIO while
maintaining strong performance under time-varying degradations and in temporal-consistency eval-
uation. The mobile demonstration indicates its potential for resource-constrained deployment.

A
ARCHITECTURE AND TRAINING DETAILS
A.1
FORWARD PASS AND MODULE INTERFACES
For each five-frame input window, the encoder produces spatially downsampled latent features and
the shallow branch retains full-resolution detail. Global pooling of the latent features and input
channel statistics provides the degradation representation and severity-related scalar. Their low-rank
interaction with the iteration encoding yields the stage condition. At each iteration, clip-level mo-
mentum and the current features determine a modulation mask; two parameter-shared LoopLayers
then update the spatio-temporal representation. Each LoopLayer has 16 routed and 2 shared experts,
with 4 routed experts activated for a clip. The attention branch precedes the expert branch, so ex-
pert modulation uses the attention-updated local state. The decoder fuses the final latent state with
shallow features and predicts a residual at the input resolution. The iteration count is selected from
{3, 4, 5} by the depth predictor.
